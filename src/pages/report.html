What are the broader impacts of a more supportive North Sea oil and gas environment?

Published May 2026 [sample date] · Independent Frontier analysis · PDF, [n] pages

Britain's energy system remains exposed to global fossil fuel markets. This second briefing looks at what a more supportive regime for North Sea production, through licensing, regulation or tax, would mean for growth, tax revenues and emissions. Part one looked at prices and resilience.

Key findings

  • Prices: more UK production would have limited impact; oil and gas prices are set internationally.
  • Resilience: some reduction in import dependence, mainly for gas. For oil the case is weaker, as most UK crude is exported.
  • Growth and jobs: no automatic boost, but it could support supply chains and help move skills to low-carbon industries.
  • Tax: receipts could rise, depending on the fiscal regime and its effect on investment.
  • Emissions: depend on what is displaced. UK gas has lower embedded emissions than LNG.
  • Climate leadership: a clear trade-off with the UK's international position.
