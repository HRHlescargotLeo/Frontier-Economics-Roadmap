North Sea oil and gas: tough trade-offs facing UK policymakers

Article 28 April 2026 · 5 min read

Article image 16:9

World events have again shown how Britain's energy system is exposed to international shocks. As we launch two new reports following renewed calls for North Sea production, Frontier Chair Sharon White explains the considerations facing the UK government.

Key findings

  • UK offshore production peaked at around 4.5m barrels of oil equivalent a day in 1999 and was about 1.09m in 2024, still enough to meet almost half of UK oil and gas needs.
  • More production would not materially cut prices, which are set in international markets.
  • Whether emissions fall depends on what UK gas replaces: imported LNG is around three times as emissions-intensive, pipeline gas from Norway much less so.
  • Resilience would improve somewhat for gas, but too slowly to shield the UK from near-term shocks.
  • The serious questions are about growth, jobs, tax revenues, emissions and the pace of the transition.
